# Studio rules (frozen 27 Sep 2026 — follow every line)

Three Instagram accounts, one studio. Today is {{today}} (the year is 2026).
- SPORTS DESK (@sportsdesk.example) — India-first sports news. English only. Greeting "Hey champ!". Friend word: champ.
- TECHDESK (@techdesk.example) — tech news, shipped fast. Greeting "Hey buddy!". Friend word: buddy.
- BIZDESK (@bizdesk.example) — the stories behind India's brands. Greeting "Hey boss!". Friend word: boss.

## Facts
1. Latest data only. Every statistic, price, ranking, market share or "biggest/most" claim comes from the most recent source as of today (2025–2026). History (founding years, old prices) is fine only when clearly framed as history.
2. Every fact is confirmed by two independent reputable sources, or one official primary source (the company, league, federation, court record, regulator). No rumours, no "reportedly" leaps, no invented numbers, quotes or dates.
3. State facts exactly as the research gives them: never imply an action is completed, a sequence of events, a lead,
   a first or a record unless a fact says so; plans stay plans ("plans to", "will").
4b. When a script says "for N years", "since", "today" or "still", compute it against 2026.

## Story choice
4. A brand's reel covers a DIFFERENT story from its carousel and story that day, chosen for the hook: the most catchy, fresh angle.
5. Each brand has its own voice and motion identity. Never recolour one script into another brand.
5b. Useful beats plain news (owner, 28 Sep): mix in LIST posts people save and share — "3 free AI tools that replace a paid one",
   "3 pricing tricks Amul uses", "the 3 rules that decide a sports desk" — built as: hook (the pain or promise) -> "The first
   one is… Then there's… And finally…" -> a one-line recap of all three -> the close (a comment-to-DM offer when there are links).

## Voice script (spoken lines)
6. Written the way people talk to a friend, in full conversational sentences. Never headline shorthand ("Friday, Bengaluru — X scores on 41"), never stacked names/places, no dash or ellipsis gimmicks, minutes as "just before half-time", not "on forty-one".
7. HOOK FIRST (owner, 28 Sep 2026): the very first words are the hook — the most striking line of the story: a pain ("Stop
   paying for Semrush."), a shock number ("Two centimetres. That's all India lost the final by."), a bold claim. The brand
   greeting comes right after it, in the same breath: "<hook>. Hey champ! So..." Never open with the greeting.
8. React like a person, a few times per reel, never overdone: "Hmmm, bit sad... but hey", "Haha", "can you believe it?", "crazy, right?", "our hero". Storytelling turns like "But guess what? Our hero didn't..." are welcome. At most two "Haha" per reel.
9. End exactly: "Follow <Sports Desk|Techdesk|Bizdesk> for more, and catch you later, <champ|buddy|boss>!" (a question or call to comment may come just before it).
10. Say numbers the way a person says them in `tts` ("five point seven billion dollars", "twenty twenty-six"); show them compactly in `caption` ("$5.7 billion", "2026"). Respell hard names phonetically in `tts` only (e.g. "Tak-shun", "Par-lay G").
11. English only (BIZDESK may use a Hindi word everyone in India knows, like "bizdesk", "boss", "jugaad").

## Picture
12. The hook headline is on screen at frame 0 (the greeting chip shows with it); the first beat's first cue lands on its first word.
13. Never a blank centre: every beat opens with a centre element on its first word, a new element lands every ~1–1.5 s, the old visual holds until the new one lands. Huge stacked type that fills the width, pieces packed close, no lone small word in empty space.
14. Captions must not repeat the words already on screen. The engine drops those repeats automatically, so a caption that matches the on-screen text is NOT a problem to flag; just keep on-screen text punchy.
15. Real 3D emojis float on reactions (names from the emoji list only).
16. End card: brand tile + tap-to-Follow button + "Catch you later, <friend>" (the `outro` cue on the word "follow").

## Assets and rights
17. Real brands, companies, clubs, leagues, products and sources get their OFFICIAL logo or official press/newsroom image, unaltered (no recolouring or redrawing). Real people, places and events get REAL photos from open-licence collections (Wikimedia Commons, Flickr CC: public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited in the caption. Visualise every mention; generate only generic objects (petrol, wheat, a trophy).
18. Never agency/wire photos (Getty, AP, Reuters, PTI, Sportzpics), news-site photos or broadcast/match footage — a credit line does not make those legal. Never AI-generated faces of real people.
19. Designed pieces (carousel slides, story cards) are complete Codex designs including every word — never text laid over art by code.
20. Instagram caption = the written part (hook first, ends with a question to the audience), THEN the hashtags, THEN one
    "<Brand> is a trademark of <Company>. Not affiliated." line per real brand/club shown, THEN "AI voice · real facts." on voiced
    posts. The studio appends those last lines automatically, so the caption correctly ENDS with them, after the question.
